# Music Reference DNA

## Purpose

Music Reference DNA defines how ReeditPro studies the audio behavior of reference videos without copying tracks, melodies, lyrics, or copyrighted music.

This is architecture only. It does not download reference audio, call external APIs, connect to Supabase, generate music, or render video.

## Core Rule

ReeditPro studies style DNA. It does not copy reference music.

The system should extract why the reference audio works, then adapt that logic to the user's own footage, story, culture/context, platform, credit budget, and approval-gated music plan.

## What ReeditPro Should Analyze

Reference Music DNA should identify:

- music cue boundaries
- number of cues
- genre/mood per cue
- lyrics vs instrumental moments
- intro music behavior
- montage music behavior
- dialogue ducking behavior
- chapter/title-card audio behavior
- transition SFX
- ambience usage
- room tone usage
- music drop/rise/resolve moments
- how music supports pacing
- how music supports captions and visual systems
- why the music works
- adaptation rules
- what not to copy

## Cue Boundary Analysis

The analyzer should locate when the reference changes musical function:

- teaser starts
- intro arrives
- first dialogue bed begins
- montage cue begins
- chapter/title card hit
- scenic breathing room
- food/social warmth
- emotional bridge
- outro resolve

Each boundary should be treated as a planning signal, not a copy instruction.

## Ambience Analysis

Reference audio should reveal when natural sound matters.

Examples:

- boat water and wind
- restaurant chatter
- room tone under dialogue
- street ambience
- footsteps
- door opens
- food/social table sounds
- villa/pool/lake ambience

Ambience should not be stripped away when it helps the viewer feel the place.

## SFX Analysis

SFX should be studied as timing and emphasis, not decoration.

Reference Music DNA can describe:

- soft whooshes
- title-card hits
- chapter transition accents
- risers
- subtle impacts
- comedic accents
- Stroke Motion draw-like sounds
- object movement sounds for Real Motion-style moments

ReeditPro should only use SFX when it improves the edit and does not overpower speech.

## Dialogue, Montage, And Outro Behavior

Dialogue sections usually need:

- instrumental-only music
- low or medium-low energy
- voice-first ducking
- no distracting lead vocal
- natural ambience preserved where useful

Montage sections can allow:

- more energy
- stronger rhythm
- language-aware vocals if no important speech is present
- bolder transitions
- cue rises and drops

Outros should:

- resolve cleanly
- avoid abrupt endings
- support final CTA or emotional close
- leave room for spoken closing if present

## Chapter And Title Card Audio

Chapter/title card moments can use:

- short music hits
- soft SFX
- small risers
- ambience transitions
- brief silence before the next section

The sound should support structure without becoming loud or gimmicky.

## Lake Como / Lifestyle Vacation Example

This is a sample reference pattern, not a hard-coded workflow and not something to copy.

Observed style direction:

- luxury lifestyle / vacation vlog
- cinematic travel visuals
- friend/group personality
- dialogue sections
- coming-up teaser
- chapter/title card moments
- boat/lake/villa/food/social scenes
- natural ambience
- subtitles/captions
- music-driven montage sections
- premium but casual feel

Music lessons to model:

- one single track is usually not enough for the full edit
- coming-up teaser can use stronger music
- dialogue sections need instrumental or very light music
- scenic travel sections need breathing room
- boat/movement sections can use more energy
- food/social sections can be warm or playful
- outro should resolve
- ambience should remain part of the experience
- chapter/title cards can use SFX or music hits
- lyrics can work in montage but not under important speech

Possible adapted cue plan:

| Cue | Purpose | Music Direction | Vocal Policy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Coming-up teaser | premium travel pulse, medium-high energy | no vocals unless no speech |
| 2 | Intro arrival | elegant cinematic lifestyle bed | instrumental only |
| 3 | Dialogue bed | soft warm pads or acoustic texture | no vocals |
| 4 | Boat/lake montage | stylish European travel groove | soft hook vocals allowed only if no speech |
| 5 | Food/social moment | warm playful lounge or acoustic cue | vocal texture only if speech-free |
| 6 | Outro resolve | calm premium closing bed | instrumental or soft non-lyrical texture |

Adaptation rules:

- keep the luxury/casual balance
- preserve location ambience
- use multiple cues only where scene changes justify them
- avoid copying any melody, lyric, track structure, or exact sound design
- avoid forcing Italian-inspired music unless the project context supports it

## What ReeditPro Must Not Copy

ReeditPro must not copy:

- original tracks
- melodies
- lyrics
- artist style too closely
- copyrighted hooks
- exact cue timing
- exact SFX sequence
- exact mix moves
- exact title-card sound design

Reference DNA should become planning guidance for a new, project-specific music brief.
